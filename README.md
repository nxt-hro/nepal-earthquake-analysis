# Earthquake Activity Analysis in Nepal (1990–2026)

An interactive Tableau dashboard analyzing **1,536 earthquake events** across Nepal over three decades, uncovering geographic patterns, seasonal trends, depth distributions, and magnitude categories.

## Project Overview

Nepal sits on the boundary of the Indian and Eurasian tectonic plates, making it one of the most seismically active regions in the world. This project explores historical earthquake data from 1990 to 2026 to identify patterns in frequency, location, depth, and magnitude — with the goal of better understanding seismic risk across the country.

---

## Key Findings

- **1,536 total earthquakes** recorded between 1990 and 2026
- **Average magnitude of 4.373**, with the strongest recorded event reaching **7.8** (the 2015 Gorkha earthquake)
- **Average depth of 21.75 km**, with the majority of earthquakes occurring in the 0–20 km shallow depth range (857 events)
- **2015 saw the highest annual frequency** with 317 earthquakes, clearly linked to the devastating April 2015 earthquake and its aftershock sequence
- **Jumla, Rikaze, and Kodri** are the top three most earthquake-prone locations in Nepal
- **Light magnitude earthquakes dominate** the distribution (~1,200 events), followed by Major (~417) and Minor categories
- The **Depth vs Magnitude scatter** shows no strong correlation — shallow earthquakes vary widely in magnitude
- **Monthly heatmap** reveals elevated activity in 2015–2016 and 2008, consistent with known major seismic events

|---|---|

| Annual Earthquake Frequency | Line chart showing yearly earthquake counts from 1990–2026 |
| Top Earthquake-Prone Locations | Horizontal bar chart of most affected cities |
| Monthly Activity Heatmap | Year × Month matrix showing seasonal and yearly patterns |
| Depth vs Magnitude Scatter | Relationship between earthquake depth and magnitude |
| Depth Distribution Bar Chart | Frequency of earthquakes across depth ranges |
| Magnitude Category Distribution | Breakdown of earthquakes by Light, Minor, Moderate, Major, Strong |
